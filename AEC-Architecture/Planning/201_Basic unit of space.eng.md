<h1 align="center">How are the basic units of space formed?</h1>
<h3 align="center">― The real criteria considered when designing rooms, corridors, lobbies, and floor-to-ceiling height</h3>

<p align="center">We experience countless spaces every day.</p>
<p align="center">Rooms at home, corridors, lobbies, and spaces with high or low ceilings.</p>
<p align="center">Yet we often vaguely wonder,</p>
<p align="center"><strong>“Why are these spaces this size?”</strong></p>
<p align="center">In architecture, spaces are not created by vague intuition.</p>
<p align="center">Spaces are shaped on top of <strong>legally permitted minimum standards</strong>,</p>
<p align="center">and are <strong>adjusted so people can actually use them comfortably</strong>.</p>

<hr />

<h2 align="center">1. Space is divided by “human behavior,” not just by area</h2>
<p align="center">The first question in design is always:</p>
<p align="center">&gt; “What do people do in this space?”</p>
<p align="center"><strong>-</strong> Sleep</p>
<p align="center"><strong>-</strong> Walk</p>
<p align="center"><strong>-</strong> Stop and wait</p>
<p align="center"><strong>-</strong> Two or more people pass at the same time</p>
<p align="center">Depending on these actions,</p>
<p align="center">each space has a <strong>minimum required size</strong> and a <strong>size that feels comfortable in use</strong>.</p>

<hr />

<h2 align="center">2. Room</h2>
<p align="center">A room is a space where <strong>surprisingly few codes directly prescribe a minimum area</strong>.</p>
<p align="center">Instead, daylighting, ventilation, and window standards are central.</p>
<p align="center"><img src="201-203_img/Room_image.jpg" alt="Room image" width="50%" /></p>

<h3 align="center">✔️ Design sense for rooms</h3>
<p align="center"><strong>-</strong> <strong>Legal standards</strong></p>
<p align="center">● Whether daylighting and ventilation can be secured is more important than the room size itself</p>
<p align="center">● Window area and placement often become the key criteria</p>
<p align="center"><strong>-</strong> <strong>Dimensions commonly used in practice</strong></p>
<p align="center">● Minimum clearance next to the bed</p>
<p align="center">● Door swing radius + passage width</p>
<p align="center">● As a result, a width of <strong>about 2.7–3.0 m or more</strong></p>
<p align="center">● is perceived as a “room that doesn’t feel cramped”</p>

<p align="center">👉 For rooms,</p>
<p align="center">what matters more than “is it legally possible”</p>
<p align="center">is <strong>“is it livable”</strong>.</p>

<hr />

<h2 align="center">3. Corridor</h2>
<p align="center">Corridors are where the gap between law and reality is most evident.</p>
<p align="center"><img src="201-203_img/Corridor_image.jpg" alt="Corridor image" width="50%" /></p>

<h3 align="center">✔️ Corridor design standards</h3>
<p align="center"><strong>-</strong> <strong>Minimum width allowed by code</strong></p>
<p align="center">● Apartments/office standards → typically <strong>around 1.2 m</strong></p>
<p align="center">● The “minimum for one person to pass”</p>
<p align="center"><strong>-</strong> <strong>Widths commonly used in practice</strong></p>
<p align="center">● <strong>Around 1.5 m</strong> → two people can pass comfortably</p>
<p align="center">● <strong>1.8 m or more</strong> → feels comfortable in shared areas</p>

<p align="center">👉 That’s why in design,</p>
<p align="center">“legally possible” and “comfortable for people” are <strong>intentionally separated</strong>.</p>

<hr />

<h2 align="center">4. Lobby</h2>
<p align="center">Lobbies rarely have strict minimum-width rules like corridors.</p>
<p align="center">Instead, the key is <strong>how many people use it at the same time</strong>.</p>

<h3 align="center">✔️ Design sense for lobbies</h3>
<p align="center"><strong>-</strong> <strong>Legal perspective</strong></p>
<p align="center">● Focus on egress, entrance width, and accessibility routes</p>
<p align="center">● Prioritize “can people move without congestion?”</p>
<p align="center"><strong>-</strong> <strong>Practical perspective</strong></p>
<p align="center">● Secure at least <strong>2.5–3.0 m or more</strong> of clear space in front of entrances</p>
<p align="center">● Plan so circulation doesn’t clog even with waiting people</p>
<p align="center">● Value the <strong>ratio of width to depth</strong> over simple area</p>

<p align="center">👉 A lobby is not just “a space that looks big,”</p>
<p align="center">it is <strong>a space where people don’t get tangled</strong>.</p>

<hr />

<h3 align="center">✔️ Standards for clear height</h3>
<p align="center"><strong>-</strong> <strong>Legal standards</strong></p>
<p align="center">● Ceiling height of a habitable room (clear height to ceiling, excluding structure and services) → legal minimum <strong>2.1 m or more</strong> (Rules on Evacuation·Fire-protection Standards for Buildings, Art. 16)</p>
<p align="center">● <strong>Clear height of about 2.3–2.4 m or more</strong> is not the legal minimum but a comfort-oriented practical recommendation</p>
<p align="center"><strong>-</strong> <strong>Heights commonly used in practice</strong></p>
<p align="center">● Residential → comfortable clear height is <strong>around 2.4–2.5 m</strong></p>
<p align="center">● Lobby/shared areas → openness starts at <strong>3.0 m or more</strong></p>

<p align="center">👉 A small change in height can completely change the impression of a space.</p>

<hr />

<h2 align="center">6. Width and proportion</h2>
<p align="center">People don’t experience spaces in “meters.”</p>
<p align="center">They experience the <strong>ratio of width to height</strong>.</p>

<h3 align="center">✔️ What designers actually look at</h3>
<p align="center"><strong>-</strong> Narrow and tall → claustrophobia, instability</p>
<p align="center"><strong>-</strong> Wide and low → stability, sometimes stuffy</p>
<p align="center"><strong>-</strong> Same area, but proportions create totally different perceptions</p>

<p align="center">👉 That’s why in design,</p>
<p align="center">after meeting legal minimums,</p>
<p align="center">we re-check <strong>how that proportion feels to people</strong>.</p>
